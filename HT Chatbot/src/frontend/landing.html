What is Human Trafficking?

Human trafficking is the recruitment, transportation, transfer, harboring or receipt of people through force, fraud or deception, with the aim of exploiting them for profit. It is a form of modern slavery and a crime under international law.

Victims can be of any age, gender, or nationality, and are often exploited for sexual purposes, forced labor, or organ harvesting. Human trafficking is a global issue that affects millions of people every year.

Types of Human Trafficking

  • Sex Trafficking: Forcing, deceiving, or coercing someone to perform commercial sex acts.
  • Labor Trafficking: Using violence, threats, lies, or other forms of coercion to force people to work.
  • Domestic Servitude: Forcing someone to work in a private household, often with restricted freedom.
  • Debt Bondage: Forcing a person to work to pay off a debt, where the terms and conditions are not clearly defined.
  • Child Trafficking: The recruitment, transportation, transfer, harboring, or receipt of children for exploitation.

Warning Signs of Human Trafficking

Recognizing the signs of human trafficking is the first step in identifying victims and helping them get the assistance they need. Here are some common indicators:

Physical Indicators

  • Signs of physical abuse or malnourishment
  • Appears exhausted, dehydrated, or showing signs of substance abuse
  • Inappropriate dress for the situation or weather
  • Untreated injuries or medical conditions

Behavioral Indicators

  • Fearful, anxious, depressed, submissive, or nervous behavior
  • Avoids eye contact and social interaction
  • Appears to be monitored or controlled by another person
  • Not allowed to speak for themselves or share personal information
  • Inconsistent personal or employment history

Environmental Indicators

  • Living with employer or in the workplace
  • Poor living conditions or multiple people in a cramped space
  • Security measures designed to keep people in (bars on windows, barbed wire)
  • Frequent movement from place to place
  • Working excessively long or unusual hours

Note: The presence of these indicators doesn't necessarily mean trafficking is occurring, but they warrant further attention and possibly reporting to authorities.

How to Report Human Trafficking

If you or someone else is in immediate danger

Call emergency services immediately:

911

(or your local emergency number)

If you suspect human trafficking but there is no immediate danger, you can report it through these channels:

In the United States

  • National Human Trafficking Hotline: 1-888-373-7888
  • Text: "BEFREE" to 233733
  • Online: humantraffickinghotline.org

Internationally

  • Contact local law enforcement
  • Reach out to local anti-trafficking organizations
  • Contact your country's national hotline (where available)
  • Report to international organizations like the UNODC or ILO

When reporting, provide as much detail as possible, including:

  • Description of the people involved
  • Locations and addresses
  • Details of any vehicles
  • Specific activities you observed
  • Dates and times

Remember: Your identity can remain anonymous when reporting.
